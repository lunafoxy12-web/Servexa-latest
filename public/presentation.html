Problem Formulation Slide 02 / 15

The Urban Logistics & On-Demand Dilemma

Why Classical Combinatorial Optimization Fails at City Scale

  • Extreme Spatiotemporal Dynamism: Requests arrive stochastically over continuous space and time; supply (couriers/technicians) constantly drifts.
  • The Combinatorial Wall: Classical maximum-weight bipartite matching (Kuhn-Munkres/Hungarian Algorithm) operates at O(N³), causing unacceptable computation latency when N > 5,000.
  • The Myopic Greedy Trap: Assigning orders solely based on instantaneous nearest-neighbor distance leaves peripheral urban zones starving of couriers and induces severe supply-demand imbalances.
  • High Stochastic Variance: Traffic congestion, weather shocks, and unpredicted customer cancellations disrupt static schedules.
// Classical Greedy Approach: const matchGreedy = (orders, providers) => orders.map(o => findNearest(o.loc, providers)); // Fails global fleet balance & long-term platform revenue!
Speaker Script: "At urban scale, optimizing solely for instantaneous travel time leads to starvation in suburban sectors and clustering in downtown corridors. We must shift from greedy heuristics to predictive multi-scale reinforcement learning."
Spatial Indexing Slide 03 / 15

Decomposing Metropolitan Spaces

Transforming Continuous Planetary Coordinates into Discrete Tractable Cells

  • Curvature Calculation: Planar Euclidean formulas distort distances; great-circle Haversine calculations are computationally required for ground truth.
  • Hierarchical Geogrid Decomposition: Dividing a metropolitan sprawl into discrete hierarchical micro-cells (resolution 7–9: ~100m–1km) and macro-regions (resolution 5–6: ~5km–10km).
  • Decoupled Parallel Matching: Partitioning enables parallel intra-cell bipartite matching while restricting cross-cell interference.
  • Boundary Crossing Mitigation: Bounding-box margin buffering ensures edge cases near cell borders are routed seamlessly without boundary dropouts.
haversine(lat1, lon1, lat2, lon2) = 2R * asin(sqrt(sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat1)*cos(lat2)*sin²(Δlon/2)))
Speaker Script: "Spatial partitioning is the prerequisite for scalable reinforcement learning. By grouping continuous GPS coordinates into discrete spatial indices, the state and action spaces become computationally tractable."
Mathematical Framework Slide 04 / 15

Markov Decision Process (MDP) for Fleet Dispatch

Mathematical Formulation of Fleet States, Actions, and Value Functions

  • State Space (S): Supply distribution, unmatched order density, average wait times, congestion indices, and temporal factors at epoch t.
  • Action Space (A): Order-to-courier match matrix and proactive fleet repositioning vectors.
  • Transition Probability P(S_{t+1} | S_t, A_t): Governed by physical courier transit, new stochastic order arrivals, and cancellation rates.
  • Reward Function R(S_t, A_t): Maximizes cumulative long-term platform gross merchandise value (GMV) while minimizing customer waiting times and courier idle deadhead mileage.
Max J(π) = E_π [ Σ_{t=0}^T γ^t · ( R_fulfillment(s_t, a_t) - λ · WaitTime(s_t, a_t) ) ]
Speaker Script: "Unlike supervised learning, RL optimizes for the sequence of decisions over time, taking short-term sacrifices in travel time to prevent future supply deficits in high-demand zones."
HRL Architecture Slide 05 / 15

The Two-Tiered Hierarchical Agent Design

Macro-Policy (Strategic Fleet Balancing) + Micro-Policy (Tactical Real-Time Matching)

  • Tier 1: Macro-Agent (High-Level Policy / Manager): Operates at larger spatial partitions (clusters/boroughs) and coarser time steps (every 5–15 minutes). It issues proactive repositioning directives to balance global supply and demand.
  • Tier 2: Micro-Agent (Low-Level Policy / Worker): Operates within individual micro-cells at instantaneous time steps (seconds). Executes local bipartite matching of arriving orders to available couriers.
  • Temporal Abstraction (Options Framework): Macro-actions represent extended sub-policies, insulating the micro-matching engine from global state bloat.
  • Elimination of Non-Stationarity: Hierarchical decomposition confines local competition, stabilizing policy gradient updates.
Speaker Script: "Hierarchical RL breaks the curse of dimensionality. The Macro-Manager redistributes the fleet between districts, while the Micro-Worker does the instantaneous order matching inside each district."

Enforcing Security Rules and Financial Serializability at the Database Layer

Future Research Slide 13 / 15

Technical Limitations & Future Horizons

Addressing Boundary-Crossing Orders, Multi-Agent Non-Stationarity, and LLM Integration

  • Boundary-Crossing Dilemma: When an order origin is in Cell A but the closest courier is just across the border in Cell B; solved via overlapping halo buffers.
  • Non-Stationarity in Multi-Agent Learning: Couriers continuously changing behavior destabilizes value estimation; addressed via centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE).
  • Cold-Start Partitions: Developing transfer learning to bootstrap newly launched city zones with sparse historical telemetry.
  • Generative AI Co-Pilots: Integrating Gemini multimodal LLMs for voice-command dispatching and autonomous natural-language customer dispute mediation.
Speaker Script: "Every real-world deployment faces edge cases: boundary friction, non-stationarity, and cold start. We mitigate boundary issues using overlapping buffer zones and integrate AI co-pilots for conversational exceptions."
